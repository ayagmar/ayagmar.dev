# 0015. Data modes for the site build

Status: accepted, 2026-10-26. Amended 2026-10-26: weather (ADR 0029), the listening history (ADR 0030), agent hours on /stats (ADR 0014) and the live part of /uses (ADR 0032) are read by the browser only. Amended 2026-10-26: build-time data is the fallback, not the truth; the browser keeps listening on the home page and /stats, and shipping and projects on /stats, current. Amended 2026-10-26: the typing panel on /stats, from `/v1/monkeytype`, is a live panel too, asked hourly (ADR 0033). Amended 2026-10-31: a tab that comes back asks every source whose last request is a minute old; shipping is asked every 5 minutes, projects every 30 and typing every 15; the listening panel and the now-playing line are rendered from one markup at build time and in the browser, with each track and artist a link to Last.fm.

## Context

The site reads snapshots at build time. Local development and tests must not depend on the live API, and a broken API must never break a deploy.

## Decision

- `SIGNALS_MODE=fixtures|api` and `SIGNALS_API_BASE` (default `https://api.ayagmar.dev`), read through `astro:env`. `fixtures` reads the `ok` snapshots in `packages/contracts/fixtures/`; `api` reads `${SIGNALS_API_BASE}/v1/<source>`.
- `just site-build` picks `api` when Workers Builds builds `main` (`WORKERS_CI_BRANCH=main`) and `fixtures` everywhere else: locally, in CI, and for Previews, which therefore never depend on the production API. Setting `SIGNALS_MODE` overrides it.
- The reader is `apps/site/src/data/reader.ts`. In `api` mode each read has a 5 second deadline covering the body, and the response is validated with the source's envelope schema from `contracts`. A failed, slow, non-JSON or invalid response logs one warning naming the URL and the reason, never the body, and the page renders its empty state. The build never fails on source data. Each source is read once per build.
- `stale` is recomputed at render time from `fetchedAt` with the per-source threshold in `contracts`, whatever the API said. A page without a script shows the time the data was fetched, in Rabat (`26 Oct, 09:00`, "Rabat time" in its title), and "May be out of date" when it was stale at build time. In `fixtures` mode, which also builds the public Previews, the envelopes are dated when the fixtures were captured (`fixturesCapturedAt`) and every panel showing them wears a strip of hazard tape reading "Sample data, not live" instead of the freshness line; `/stats` opens with a taped notice that the page shows the test fixtures and links to the live one. The shipping fixtures are invented, and a Preview was once read as live.
- Build-time data is the fallback, not the truth (amended 2026-10-26). Production rebuilds only on merges, so a snapshot read at build time ages by hours or days while signals refreshes every minute. The build still renders every panel, as the first paint and for readers without JavaScript; in the browser, `src/lib/live.ts` keeps a panel current when it carries `data-live="<source>"`, `data-fetched-at` (the build's snapshot, absent when there was none) and `data-stale-after` (the source's threshold from `contracts`), plus, in `api` mode only, `data-live-src="${SIGNALS_API_BASE}/v1/<source>"`. The panels: listening on the home page (the now-playing line, `src/scripts/now-playing.ts`) and on `/stats`, and shipping, projects and typing on `/stats` (`src/scripts/stats-live.ts`).
- Polling (amended 2026-10-31): on load, then while the tab is visible every minute for listening (pulled every minute), every 5 minutes for shipping (pulled every 15), every 30 for projects (pulled every 6 hours) and every 15 for typing (pulled hourly, ADR 0033), so a panel is never more than its pull interval plus its asking interval behind the upstream. One request per panel at a time, each with a 5 s deadline covering the body, no cache-busting (the route caches 60 s at the edge). A hidden tab asks for nothing. One that comes back (`visibilitychange`, `focus`, `pageshow` from the back/forward cache) relabels at once and asks every source whose last request is at least a minute old, whatever its own interval: before, a tab hidden for ten minutes showed what it had until its next 15-minute turn, and only a reload asked at once. The minute keeps a reader switching windows from asking on every focus. `poll` and `due` in `src/lib/live.ts` hold the rule, and `test/poll.test.ts` checks it with a fake clock, visibility and network; the history's own poller (ADR 0030) uses the same `due`. Requests at most: one a minute per open, visible tab for listening, twelve an hour for shipping, four for typing, two for projects, against Workers Free's 100,000 a day (ADR 0010). Tabs share nothing; each asks on its own and the edge cache absorbs the overlap.
- An answer replaces what a panel shows when it is a valid envelope of that source (version 1, a `stale` flag, a `fetchedAt` the contract accepts, calendar-checked, at most 2 minutes ahead of the reader's clock), its data passes the source's browser reader (`src/lib/listening.ts`, `src/lib/stats.ts`, `src/lib/typing.ts`, the contract's rules written out, held to the zod schemas by `test/live.test.ts`), and it is current: not flagged `stale` by signals, within the source's threshold of the reader's clock, and at least as new as what is shown. Anything else leaves the panel as it is, the build's snapshot included: a failed, slow, malformed, never-succeeded, other-source, future, older or stale answer, even a stale one newer than what is shown. The panel then keeps its content and lets the label age. Every panel's line reads "Updated 3 minutes ago", or "May be out of date: updated 7 hours ago" once what it shows is older than the threshold, and it keeps ageing while the page is open.
- Times read as a person says them, in the reader's own time zone: "just now", "3 minutes ago", "5 hours ago", "yesterday" (a different calendar date, after 6 hours), "3 days ago", then the date; the `<time>` keeps the instant in `datetime` and the exact local time in `title`, and the page ticks them every minute. Each sits in a box as wide as the longest of these texts, so rewriting it never reflows the page (CLS budget, ADR 0017).
- Now playing: "Now playing <track> by <artist>" only while Last.fm says so in an answer that still describes now: fresh by `fetchedAt` and the listening threshold (15 minutes), not flagged stale, and brought by the latest request (a failed refresh, or any answer that does not replace what is shown, such as an older or stale one, ends it). Otherwise "Last played <track> by <artist> · <when>": the track Last.fm last saw playing at `fetchedAt`, or the newest scrobble at its `playedAt`. With neither: "Nothing playing right now" while the answer describes now, else "Nothing played lately · checked <when>", so an old empty answer is never an undated claim. The build cannot know, so it always says "Last played". The sentence is a polite live region, announced whole when it changes; the ticking time is outside it, after a dot that wraps with it (`.when`), so a line never ends on a comma with the time alone below. The track and the artist each link to their Last.fm page. Links go only to `https://www.last.fm/`: a link in the answer is used only if the reader finds it there (an answer with any other link fails the contract), and a missing one is built from the names (`src/lib/lastfm.ts`, Last.fm's own path encoding, checked against its pages on 2026-10-31 and held by `test/lastfm.test.ts`). This replaces the Svelte now-playing island: one renderer for the home page and `/stats`, and no framework added to either page.
- One markup for what both draw (amended 2026-10-31): the listening panel's body (`src/lib/listening-panel.ts`: the week's scrobbles, recent tracks and top artists, each track and artist a link to Last.fm) and the now-playing line (`nowPlayingMarkup` in `src/lib/listening.ts`) are built once as plain data (`src/lib/markup.ts`), which `Markup.astro` renders at build time and `toNode` in the browser, so a live answer cannot render differently from the build. Text stays text in both. The shipping, projects and typing panels still have a renderer each in `stats.astro` and `stats-live.ts`, held together by `e2e/stats-live.spec.ts`.
- `connect-src` allows only `https://api.ayagmar.dev` (ADR 0019), so with another `SIGNALS_API_BASE` the build reads work but the browser's requests are blocked, and the panels keep the build's snapshot.
- Weather is the exception to build-time reads (ADR 0029): the build never reads `/v1/weather` and no page renders it, since a condition read at build time would stay on the page until the next merge. In `api` mode the home page's `.scene` carries `data-weather-src="${SIGNALS_API_BASE}/v1/weather"` and the room script reads it in the browser; in `fixtures` mode there is no attribute, no request and no weather, and no sample weather is shown as current.
- The listening history behind "On this day" is the second exception (ADR 0030): the build never reads `/v1/listening-history`. `/stats` has the block `hidden` in its HTML; in `api` mode it carries `data-history-src` and the page's script shows a snapshot only while its `describedDate` is today's date in Rabat. In `fixtures` mode there is no attribute, no request and no block.
- `llm-usage` and `desktop` are also read by the browser only, for the same reason: `/stats` shows estimated agent hours (ADR 0014) and `/uses` the desktop facts and this month's agents (ADR 0032), and a build-time uptime or "this month" would stay on the page until the next merge. Each block is `hidden` in the HTML; in `api` mode it carries `data-llm-usage-src` or `data-desktop-src`, and `src/lib/live.ts` reads the endpoint on load and every 30 minutes while the tab is visible, one request at a time with a 5 s deadline. Each block is checked again every minute and when the tab comes back, and shows only while its answer holds: agent hours and agents on the date in Rabat the snapshot was taken, the desktop facts for 2 days from their report. A failed request keeps the last good answer until it stops holding. Each says when its snapshot was taken ("Updated 4 hours ago", "Reported 2 hours ago") as a relative time like the panels above. In `fixtures` mode there is no attribute, no request and no block. The static part of `/uses` (`content/uses.json`) renders whatever the reads do.
- `shelf` renders its empty state from `emptyEnvelope()` until its schema exists; `llm-usage`'s tokens and costs are not shown yet.

## Consequences

A deploy whose build ran while signals was down shows empty sections to readers without JavaScript until the next build; with it, the first good answer fills them in. Pages without a live panel (projects, a post's project card, the home shipping list) still show build-time numbers, with the time they were fetched; the home shipping list's times read relative to now but its events are the build's. Each open tab costs signals Worker requests (ADR 0010 has the count).
