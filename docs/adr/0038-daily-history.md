# 0038. Daily history

Status: accepted, 2026-11-01. Amended 2026-11-01 before release: shipping days merge per repository and count instead of by one total, 35 days hourly and 400 once a UTC date, a second request after an incomplete answer, each pull's attempt recorded before it runs, days bound to the snapshot write that brought them, and `last=28` cached only until midnight. Amended 2026-11-06: `readHistory` names the months that failed, for the year pages (ADR 0041). Amended 2026-11-06: `listening` and `weather` join the table, with their own merge rules and a bounded backfill; see "Listening and weather". Amends ADR 0009 (a seventh adapter), ADR 0010 (a table, a route, budgets), ADR 0014 (llm-usage days are also kept in history) and ADR 0034 (the shipping strip's data). Amended 2026-11-06: the site reads `listening` and `weather` too, for the room's scrub (ADR 0044). Amended 2026-11-07: a listening pull's own reads are fewer, see ADR 0009; the hold covers its slow parts too, the day reads and their window are unchanged. Amended 2026-11-11: the activity pull asks for 400 days only after a pull that stored its answer, so a 400-day answer that keeps failing no longer stops the series; see Consequences.

## Context

The shipping strip on the home page and /stats drew 28 days from the shipping snapshot's 50 newest events. On 1 November those reached back only to 30 October (44 merges on the 31st), so 25 of the 28 squares were empty while the caption said "5 Oct to 1 Nov". signals keeps only the latest snapshot per source, so nothing older survived.

repo-pulse now serves `GET /public/activity?days=N` (ayagmar/repo-pulse#38): public activity summed per UTC day, with a per-repository breakdown. signals should keep such days for good, as the base for a later `/day/<date>` page and per-post or per-repository receipts. This decision wires the shipping activity and the llm-usage push's days; other sources can join the same table later.

## Decision

### The table

`apps/signals/migrations/0004_daily.sql` adds `daily (source, day, json, fetched_at, updated_at)`, `STRICT, WITHOUT ROWID`, primary key `(source, day)`. Rows are inserted or replaced, never deleted. `0005_snapshot_write_id.sql` adds a nullable `snapshots.write_id` (below); it is a migration of its own because the shared preview database already ran the first version of 0004. Nothing else changes, and the Worker version before it runs unchanged against both.

A series' days are in its own zone, given by `dailyZones` in `packages/contracts/src/days.ts`: `shipping-activity` counts UTC days (repo-pulse's), `llm-usage`, `listening` and `weather` days in Africa/Casablanca (ADR 0014). A Rabat day cannot be turned into a UTC day from daily totals, so neither is converted. A later `/day/<date>` page shows each series under its own date with its zone named.

### Writers

**`shipping-activity`** is a source of its own (`sources.ts`, stale after 6 hours) with a pull adapter, `apps/signals/src/adapters/pull/shipping-activity.ts`, rather than a part of `shipping`: as its own source its failures show in `/v1/health` (until repo-pulse deploys the route, `upstream_not_found`), and an activity outage cannot blank the shipping list. Hourly, it asks for `days=35`, and for `days=400` on its first pull of each UTC date (when the snapshot's `fullOn` is not today and the last pull stored its answer), body capped at 512 KiB. The 400 days backfill history on the first success and catch late changes to older days once a day; the 35 cover yesterday's late events and today at a tenth of the CPU (budgets below). The answer (schema `repo-pulse-activity.input.json`) is all or nothing, since what it writes is kept for good:

- `complete: false` (repo-pulse's visibility gate left a repository unresolved and the counts may be short) is asked for once more at once, within the pull's 20-second deadline: the gate stays warm for 60 seconds, so the second answer usually completes. A second `complete: false` is `upstream_incomplete`, nothing is stored, and the pull is due again 2 minutes later instead of an hour. Other failures keep the hourly schedule.
- It must be exactly 35 or 400 days, one per date, oldest first, ending on the UTC date of `generatedAt`; each day's repositories sorted and listed once; each day's counts the sums of its repositories', or at least that when `reposTruncated`. `since` must not be after the last day, every day before it must be empty (zero counts, no repositories), and with `since: null` every day must be. Anything else, a bad field included, is `upstream_malformed`. A `generatedAt` more than 5 minutes ahead of the Worker's clock is too.
- 404, 429, 503 and other statuses fail as the feed's do (ADR 0009, ADR 0012).
- Days before `since` (when repo-pulse began recording) are left out, never stored as zeros. `since: null` is a success that writes no day.

The snapshot is small: `since`; `repos`, the repositories that may be named when history is served; and `fullOn`, the UTC date of the last 400-day answer stored. A 400-day answer sets `repos` to those it names on any day. A 35-day answer adds those it names and drops those a stored day of its window lists but the answer names nowhere: made private, renamed or deleted. Its `fetched_at` is repo-pulse's `generatedAt`, which orders answers.

`putDated` reads the stored snapshot and the stored days of the answer's dates, then writes in one D1 batch, only if the stored answer is strictly older (an equally dated answer loses whole, so the first one stored wins): the merged snapshot with a new random `write_id`, taken only if the row still carries the `write_id` it read; the days that changed, only if the row now carries the new `write_id`; and the attempt, either way. So days are written only with the snapshot of the answer that brought them, and a pull that read a state another pull has since replaced writes nothing; the next pull brings its news again. A slow or overlapping pull whose answer is older than the stored one is a successful pull that changes nothing.

The adapter avoids the first 15 minutes of a date in Rabat (`dueAt`), which belong to the listening history (ADR 0030); its worst case at midnight stays at 10 minutes 2.3 seconds.

**`llm-usage`** keeps the push as it is. `admitPush` adds a third statement to its batch that reads the days from the snapshot just stored, not from the request, and runs only when `snapshots.last_delivery_id` is this delivery and `push_deliveries` records this producer, delivery id and body hash. A retry, a reused id with another body, a too-soon or an older push writes no day. Once the series has any row, only the push's newest 35 days are written; the first accepted push writes all of its days (up to 371).

### What wins for a day

A smaller count for a past day means data was lost or left out, not that less happened: repo-pulse drops a repository made private, counts and all, and llm-usage-metrics reads agent logs that some agents delete after a month. So stored counts never go down.

**Shipping** days are merged in `mergeDay`: each repository keeps, for each of the five counts, the larger of the stored and the new value, and a repository the new answer leaves out keeps what it had. Each day count is the largest of the stored day's, the new day's and the merged repositories' sum, so late activity for one repository lands while another's hidden counts stay. When more than 50 repositories result, the 50 busiest are listed. `reposTruncated` is true when the counts include repositories not listed. A day whose merge changes nothing is not written.

**llm-usage** days are replaced only by a report at least as new (`fetched_at`) with other values and at least as many `totalTokens`. Ties replace, so a repriced cost or a newly reported `activeMs` lands.

### Read route

`GET /v1/days/<series>?month=YYYY-MM` or `?last=28`, series `shipping-activity`, `llm-usage`, `listening` or `weather`. A month runs from 2025-01 to the current month in the series' zone; `last=28` is the 28 days ending today there. Anything else is `400 {"error":"invalid_query"}`; another series is the JSON 404. Only these blocks exist, so each is one edge cache entry, cached as the snapshot routes are (ADR 0010). `last=28` moves at midnight in the series' zone, so its cache key carries the date it ends on and no copy is kept, at the edge or in a browser, past that midnight: a site built just after it draws the new window.

The answer is a contract envelope (`days-shipping-activity.output.json`, `days-llm-usage.output.json`, frozen in `test/frozen/v1/`) with `fetchedAt` and `stale` from the source's snapshot, and `data` null until the source has succeeded once, else `{ zone, from, to, days }`: the days with a record, oldest first, one per date. A date in the block without an entry was not recorded. Sorting and uniqueness are guarantees of the route, tested there, not of the JSON Schema.

A shipping day lists only repositories that are the owner's and are named in the latest activity answer (repo-pulse names public repositories only) or are featured projects. Another repository's counts stay in the day and its name is left out, and `reposComplete` is false, as it is when repo-pulse listed only the 50 busiest. A repository made private disappears from every day at the next pull if it was active in the last 35 days, and otherwise at the next 400-day pull, within a day.

### The strip

The home page and /stats draw the strip once, at build, from `/v1/days/shipping-activity?last=28` (`shippingStrip` in `apps/site/src/data/format.ts`, `ShippingHeat.astro`). A square counts the day's pushes, merges and releases; stars are attention, not shipping. Days without a record are drawn as an outline, at least 3:1 against the strip (`e2e/contrast.spec.ts`), and the caption names the first and last day with a record and, when some are outlined, says so. Under it: "Counted up to 26 Oct, 08:55 UTC", the history's `fetchedAt`, since the list's live freshness line belongs to another source and the strip is never redrawn in the browser.

Without the route, or with no recorded day in the 28, the strip falls back to the events of the shipping snapshot, over the days from the oldest event's to the snapshot's own (UTC), ending on the build's day, and counted up to the snapshot's `fetchedAt`. Previews draw the fixture (ADR 0015), where recording begins on 12 October and 13 days are outlined.

### Listening and weather

The room will scrub through past days (ADR 0044), and both sources kept only a snapshot that each pull replaced, so every day that passed was lost. Both are series of `/v1/days` now, in `Africa/Casablanca`, the room's own days. No migration: `daily.source` is text, and the `listening` and `weather` snapshots keep their shape. Their rows are **day summaries**: they say what a date came to, not what played or what the sky did at any instant in it, and a past scrobble never means "playing".

**A listening day** is `{ count, last, asOf }`. `count` is Last.fm's `total` for the date's range, `rabatDayStart(date)` to `rabatDayStart(nextDate(date))`, read with `limit=1`; `last` is the newest dated scrobble in that range, `{ track, artist, playedAt }`, which is what the record needs (the site builds the link from the names); the track playing now is left out. `asOf` is when the request began, so a read that crossed midnight counts as open: a day with `asOf` before its own end is a count so far. The answer goes through the page check `summarizeDay` uses (`readRangePage`), never the ten recent tracks, which can prove neither a count nor a zero. A checked empty range is `{ count: 0, last: null }`; a failed request, an error body or a page that fails a check writes nothing.

**When.** A listening pull in the first 5 minutes of an hour (`dayReadsDue`) also reads today and yesterday, in parallel with its own requests. Outside the window it is the plain `put` it was, with no history and no extra read. So no new adapter or trigger exists and the runner picks as before: ADR 0030's midnight timing is unchanged, and `test/cron.test.ts` still asserts it, with a backfill armed, and asserts that every hour of the two simulated days has a pull in the window across both midnights. Reading yesterday for all of the next date takes in late scrobbles and a day or so of outage; anything later is what the backfill is for. A failed read waits for the next window rather than asking every minute, with no state to keep. A day's failure never costs the pull its snapshot.

**Whether days are being kept.** A snapshot that works says nothing about days that do not, so each attempt's outcome is stored apart: a `days:<series>` row in `pull_progress`, which needs no migration and which an older Worker never reads. `/v1/health` serves it as `days`, per series, beside `sources`: `lastSuccessAt`, `stale` (no recording for 3 hours for listening, 45 minutes for weather), `lastErrorCode`, `lastErrorAt`, `lastErrorDate` and `retryAfterAt`. Codes are the fixed `PullError` ones. A pull with any date unread records a failure and keeps `lastSuccessAt`; only a pull whose every date recorded clears the error, and it counts only if its fenced write was taken, in the same batch. A pull outside the window says nothing either way, so it cannot clear one. What is missing stays missing: this reports, and writes no day.

**A refused day read.** The pull's own requests can succeed while its day reads are refused, and storing that snapshot clears the source's cooldown, so a refusal's time is kept with the recording: `retryAfterAt` in the `days:listening` row, the longest of all reads and of what was held, and only passing it ends it. Until then no live day read asks and no backfill takes a turn; the snapshot is stored as usual. A Last.fm refusal the backfill meets is held there too, without counting as a recording failure.

**A weather day** is `{ day, night }`, each null or `{ code, at, origin }`: the WMO code nearest 13:00 with the sun up, and nearest 22:00 with it down, in Rabat (the sun is up at the first and down at the second all year; both are hours after `rabatDayStart`, so a clock change that day moves them an hour). Every forecast pull offers its reading to the slot its `isDay` names, at no extra request; a reading with no `isDay` fills none, and neither does one dated more than `maxUpstreamAheadMs` (5 minutes, the limit the runner holds any upstream date to) past the Worker's clock, which would otherwise sit at its slot's aim for good, as no real reading could beat it and an archived one may not. Null is unknown, never clear, and a row with both null is not written. Codes are stored as Open-Meteo gave them. The one table moved to `@min7/contracts/weather-codes`, zod-free, so the browser still loads it alone and the merge can tell a known code from an unknown one.

**What wins for a day.** Each series merges in `mergedRows` with its own rule, in the same fenced batch as its snapshot (`putDated`):

- Listening: the count never goes down, as ADR 0038's other series. The last track is ordered by `playedAt`, never by arrival, independently of the count, so an equal count still admits a later track; at the same `playedAt` the newer read wins, which lets a corrected title land. `asOf` moves on while a day is open. A read that changes nothing, over a row already read after the date's end, writes nothing, so a closed day stops being rewritten.
- Weather: slots merge on their own. A known code beats an unknown one, whatever its time; then live beats archive; then the instant nearest the slot's aim; a tie keeps what is stored. Nothing empties a slot.
- A stored row this version cannot read is left alone and logged.

**Block freshness.** A block's `fetchedAt` and `stale` come from the source's snapshot as for the other series, so they say how recently the source was collected, not that an old day is complete. `asOf`, and a slot's `at`, say that. `/v1/days/listening` and `/v1/days/weather` have their own frozen schemas (`days-listening.output.json`, `days-weather.output.json`); no earlier frozen file changed. The site reads both for the room's scrub (ADR 0044): `DaysBlocks` in `apps/site/src/data/reader.ts` lists what it reads.

### Backfill

`just signals-backfill <from> <to>` arms a one-off in `pull_progress` under its own row, `daily-backfill`, and `src/cron/backfill.ts` works through it. It runs in the Worker because the Worker holds the Last.fm key, so nothing secret is copied or typed; the cron is what makes it resumable. docs/operations.md has the procedure.

- **Unit.** One date per turn: 1 Last.fm request, then 1 archive request, both or nothing before anything commits, in at most 10 seconds.
- **Its turn.** A date takes a cron run's turn in place of a pull, so adapters and a backfill never share an invocation: a pull near the CPU limit and a date together could lose the date every time, and spend its tries with nothing wrong upstream. It takes the turn only when the most overdue adapter is under 30 seconds overdue, so nothing waits behind it, listening runs at least every other minute, and a date takes about two minutes. It takes none in the first 15 minutes of a date in Rabat, the window ADR 0030 protects (`midnightYieldMs`). "A failed pull writes nothing" holds per date, not across the range. A run killed between the two series' commits repeats the date, and the merges make that change nothing.
- **Bounds.** `to` must be before today in Rabat, `from` no earlier than `daysFirstMonth`, and the range at most 400 days; anything else is refused without a request. Each try is counted before any work, as `recordAttempt` does, so a run the CPU limit kills counts too: after 5 tries on a date the backfill stops, keeps `next` and the last `code`, and asks nothing until armed again.
- **Pacing.** A failed try stores `notBefore`: the upstream's `Retry-After`, or 5 minutes. It is in the state, so a restart keeps it, and a run before it asks nothing and spends no try. A cooldown the runner holds for `listening`, `listening-history` or `weather` (the same two upstreams) holds the backfill the same way, and so does a refusal of a day read, below.
- **What finishes a date.** Listening read, and both weather slots present, from history or this answer. A slot still null commits what there is and retries in an hour, as the archive updates every 6; 5 such tries stop the backfill with `upstream_incomplete`, the date reported incomplete and never done. An answer without the two hours is about another date and fails as `upstream_malformed`; only a null code means "not yet".
- **Snapshots.** It never writes a snapshot's JSON, times or error: `/v1/listening` and `/v1/weather` cannot show history as current, or look fresher for it, and a date older than the snapshot is the normal case, not a skipped one. `mergeDays` moves only `write_id`, the fence `putDated` already uses, so a backfill and a live pull that read the same state cannot overwrite each other: the second to write changes nothing. A live pull that lost comes back within the hour for listening, 15 minutes for weather; a backfill that lost retries the date.
- **Archive.** Checked on 2026-11-06. Open-Meteo's historical docs (https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api) list `https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive` with `hourly` `weather_code`, ECMWF IFS at "9 km Hourly 2017 to present, Every 6 hours with no delay" and ERA5 at "Daily with 5 days delay". The terms are those of ADR 0029, unchanged in wording: "Less than 10'000 API calls per day, 5'000 per hour and 600 per minute", non-commercial, CC BY 4.0. One keyless request per date, a minute apart, is far inside them. Requests for 2026-10-01 and 2026-11-04 returned all 24 hours, for the cell at 33.989 N, 6.854 W, not the forecast's 34.0044 N, 6.8209 W. That is why a slot records its `origin` and an archived code never replaces a live one: it is another model's answer for another cell, and only fills what signals missed. The credit stays as ADR 0029 has it; whichever page first draws these days adds nothing to the changes it lists but the choice of one reading per slot.
- **Last.fm.** Its page (https://www.last.fm/api/show/user.getRecentTracks) gives `from` and `to` as UTC Unix seconds; ADR 0030 records the bounds as checked live. A date is one request whatever its count, so the range's cost is its length. No Last.fm request was made for this change: `limit=1` with `to` is what the weekly totals already send, but `perPage` is checked equal to 1 here and that value has not been seen live. Read `day read failed` in Workers Logs after the first deploy.

## Consequences

- Listening and weather history begin at the first deploy, or at the backfill's `from`. A date no pull and no backfill reached has no row. Weather days filled from the archive say so.
- A day's weather is two readings. It cannot say it rained at 09:00 if it was clear at 13:00 and 22:00, and it is not meant to.
- A scrobble deleted on Last.fm leaves the count where it was, and one that arrives more than a day late is missed unless a backfill covers its date.
- During a backfill a live weather or listening write that lost the fence is dropped for that run, and listening pulls about every other minute. Nothing else in the snapshot row moves.
- The strip shows only days signals has recorded. repo-pulse began recording on 3 October 2026 and cannot seed older days, so from the first successful pull the 28 days are covered; a day or month before 3 October has no record.
- History is as good as repo-pulse's `complete` flag and visibility gate: no count of a day stored from a complete answer is lowered later.
- A repository renamed after a day was stored is counted under both names on that day, since nothing tells a rename from a repository made private and another active. This account renames rarely; the fix is a manual D1 statement.
- The 400-day pull costs about what every pull did before this amendment; it now runs once a day. Workers ends a cron invocation over the CPU limit without running its error handling, so the runner records each pull's attempt before the adapter does any work (`recordAttempt`): a pull cut off that way is due again after its interval, not at every run, and cannot keep the other sources from being pulled. A `shipping-activity` pull asks for 400 days only when the last attempt stored its answer. After a failure (its code stays through later attempts until a success) or an attempt cut off that way, it asks for 35. So if the 400 days are too many for production, or one old day keeps failing a check, the 400 days are tried every other pull and the series still advances. Until one succeeds, a repository made private whose activity is older than 35 days stays named. An attempt whose answer was not newer also leads to a 35-day pull and costs only one hour's delay of that date's 400-day pull. Watch `shipping-activity` in Workers Logs after the first deploy.
- The rollout has an order (docs/operations.md): migrate, deploy signals, check the route has days, then rebuild the site. A site built before the route answers keeps the event fallback until its next build.
- The site reads every month block of both series at build for `/day/YYYY-MM-DD` pages and receipt lines (`apps/site/src/lib/days.ts`): two reads per month served, one cache entry each. A page shows each series under its own date and names its zone; the strip's squares link to those pages and fold into two rows of 14, or four of 7, wherever 28 links in a row would sit closer than 24 px (WCAG 2.5.8).
- Rows are never deleted. Removing a series, or a day, is a migration or a manual D1 statement.
